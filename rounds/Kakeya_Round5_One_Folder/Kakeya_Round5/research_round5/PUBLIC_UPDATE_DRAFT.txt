We have a new partial result in an exact finite Kakeya problem over the
two four-element local rings, Z/4Z and F2[epsilon]/(epsilon^2).

The dimension-five target is 94 points. **We have not proved that lower
bound in full.** What we can now exclude is every hypothetical smaller
example whose points occupy at most ten pair-active residue fibres.
The support-ten theorem has **no restriction on the largest fibre**.

The mechanism is concrete. Every ten-point residue difference cover has
the same affine form: seven nonzero points of a three-space together
with three nonzero points of a complementary two-space. That forces
24 residue directions to have a unique supporting edge. Those edges
must each cover a whole 16-point quotient.

Pair counting, a three-point sumset lemma, and Mantel rigidity then force
a contradiction. A sixteen-point central fibre becomes an affine
four-flat but needs five-dimensional direction span. The remaining
seventeen-point case becomes a four-flat plus one point and cannot cover
its other required directions. The weight argument is analytic; the
support classification has separately checked finite certificates.

Across the ongoing middle search, this removes 4,976 further weight
profiles and closes the whole support-ten layer. Larger supports remain
open. We also independently established the 38 marked support cases
underlying the other session's reported maximum-19 closure, and built
a verified-checker acceptance route. The actual new encoder and proof
files still need to pass through that route.

The result gives a sharper structural theorem and a precise, auditable
description of the cases that remain open.
