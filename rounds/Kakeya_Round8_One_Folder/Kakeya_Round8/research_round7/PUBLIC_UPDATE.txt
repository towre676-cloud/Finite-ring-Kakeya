We now have a complete elementary proof of an exact finite-ring Kakeya
result: for both the integers modulo 4 and the dual numbers over F2,
the smallest Kakeya set in dimension 3 has 22 points.

The question is concrete: how few points can contain a complete four-point
line in every primitive direction? Reducing modulo the nilpotent element
divides the problem into eight binary residue boxes. A22-point construction
is simple: fill one box and put two appropriately chosen points in each
of the other seven. The work is proving that 21 points cannot suffice.

The proof combines pair counting with geometry. Large fibres can look
efficient in a scalar energy estimate, but their useful directions must
fit together across actual edges. A short heavy/light charging argument,
plus one shared-plane obstruction, closes every support size.

There is also a separate computational route: all five marked support
classes, in each of the two rings, have exact actual-point encodings and
checked refutations. These computations corroborate the elementary proof;
the proof does not depend on SAT or a spectrum census.

The same charging idea has now excluded 3,006 entire weight profiles in
our dimension 5 search, reducing the middle-maxima list from 186,482 to
183,476. New support 13 certificates also close another whole maximum 11
affine class. The dimension 5 target 94 and the general formula 3*2^n-2
remain open.

The methodological gain is useful beyond this one count: replace a loose
global energy estimate by explicit prices for the resources that edges
actually consume, then keep an exact integer certificate for every exclusion.
The checkpoint includes the elementary proof, code, certificates, precise
remaining-case ledger, and an exactly 1,500-word explanation.
