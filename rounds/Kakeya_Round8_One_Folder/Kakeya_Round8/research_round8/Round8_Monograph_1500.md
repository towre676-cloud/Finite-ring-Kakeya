# Round Eight: A Complete Branch and a Reusable Method

**We have closed the maximum-eleven, support-thirteen branch of the dimension-five problem in both rings.** Four remaining placements now have complete refutation proofs, checked against their exact formulas. Together with the inherited reductions, they exclude every possible fibre shape and every largest-fibre position in that branch. Three affine support classes disappear from the open ledger, reducing the maximum-eleven list from forty-five classes to forty-two. The full ninety-four-point lower bound remains open. This distinction gives the result its proper scale: a complete, reproducible exclusion inside a larger unfinished theorem.

The problem asks how small a set can be while containing a line in every primitive direction. Our ambient spaces are five-dimensional over either the integers modulo four or the binary dual-number ring. Reducing coordinates modulo the maximal ideal leaves thirty-two binary residues. Above each residue sits a fibre, itself a subset of binary five-space. Its size is its weight. A residue is pair-active when its fibre has at least two points. The support counts these active residues. The maximum is the largest fibre weight, with ties allowed throughout the argument.

This language separates two sources of complexity. Fibre shapes determine how many pairs point in each binary direction. The residue support determines which fibres can cooperate along that direction. If two residues differ by a nonzero vector d, their fibres supply quotient pair-sets in a sixteen-point space. Their sumset covers some lifted directions. A Kakeya set must cover all sixteen lifts for each of the thirty-one nonzero binary directions. The earlier relaxation tracked many valid local resources. Round Eight adds restrictions on how those resources fit the actual support geometry.

**The first new theorem concerns heavy fibres**, meaning fibres with at least four points. A thirteen-residue configuration with maximum at most eleven needs at least ten heavy fibres, and the heavy residues must realize every nonzero difference. This theorem uses neither the ninety-three-point budget nor an exhaustive fibre census. Small fibres have only one pair in any given direction, while every partner has at most five. Missing directions between heavy residues therefore impose severe demands on the few small fibres. Those demands must also respect the elementary geometry of their pair directions.

Here is the key obstruction. Nine binary residues miss at least three differences. If only nine fibres were heavy, four small fibres would have to cover every missing direction. Three active small fibres could supply at most fifteen lifts, so all four would have to participate. A three-point fibre has exactly three pair directions, forming a binary plane. The missing directions would consequently contain that plane. But a heavy set avoiding all three plane directions can occupy at most one point in each of eight plane cosets. Nine heavy residues are impossible under that condition.

Once there are ten heavy fibres, only three small fibres remain. Their combined capacity in any direction is at most fifteen. Every direction therefore requires a heavy-heavy edge. This turns a loose size restriction into an explicit condition on the heavy residue set. In the four remaining marked supports, the allowed heavy subsets number thirty-seven, fifty, thirty-eight, and thirty-nine. These counts describe possible heavy labels inside each support, rather than complete Kakeya configurations. They provide compact Boolean restrictions while preserving every genuine candidate.

The second improvement sharpens an individual edge. Two nonplanar four-point sets in binary four-space have a sumset of size at most fourteen. Each set spans a three-space and misses only one nonzero difference inside that space. Their spans intersect in at least a plane, leaving a common difference. Two distinct collisions then occur among the sixteen ordered sums. Certain eight-point and eleven-point fibre types always produce these tetrahedral quotient sets when their directional pair count is four. The new encoder applies the fourteen bound on every eligible edge, including directions supported by several edges.

A separate argument strengthens the structural picture further: below ninety-four points, thirteen fibres of maximum at most eleven would require **at least eleven heavy fibres**. Ten heavy residues covering every difference have a rigid shape: a punctured three-space together with a punctured complementary two-space, after translation. A complete classification checks fourteen thousand nine hundred fifty normalized ten-point sets. Eighty are difference covers, and every one has that shape. Normalization is complete because any such set contains an affine basis; uniqueness of the chosen basis is unnecessary.

That shape forces twenty-four directions to have exactly one heavy-heavy edge. The three vertices in the smaller component require fibres of affine rank five. The most useful extension reaches the middle maxima. Let M be the directions missing between heavy residues, with at least nine heavy residues. Then M contains no complete binary plane. A two-point fibre contributes to at most one direction of M; a three-point fibre contributes to at most two. Each missing direction needs enough active small fibres to supply sixteen lifts. This forces ten heavy fibres for support thirteen with maximum at most fifteen, and support fourteen with maximum at most eleven. **Distinct partners sharpen this further.** Edges in one direction form a matching, so different small fibres must use different heavy partners.
