Hyperplane crossing-pair budgets
===============================

Let A be a w-element subset of V=F_2^n.  For nonzero d let p_A(d)
be its number of unordered pairs with difference d.  Let u:V->F_2
be a nonzero linear functional and put a=|A intersect ker(u)|.
Then

    sum_{u(d)=1} p_A(d) = a(w-a) <= floor(w^2/4).

Indeed, a pair has u(d)=1 exactly when its two endpoints lie on
opposite sides of the hyperplane ker(u).  There are a(w-a) such pairs.
The inequality follows from (2a-w)^2 >= 0.  In particular, the same
upper bound holds when only some of the directions are summed.

This is an elementary statement about one binary fibre.  It uses no
spectrum census, Kakeya assumption, support catalogue, or SAT solver.
It holds for fibres belonging to either of the two rings under study.

The Round8 wrapper uses only existing directional count selectors y and
fibre spectrum selectors z.  It defines shared unary thresholds
q(v,d,j) iff p_v(d)>=j, takes a bidirectional totalizer over the
thresholds with u(d)=1, and adds

    selected_spectrum_i -> crossing_total <= floor(w_i^2/4).

Set all new thresholds and counter outputs to their actual values for
a real fibre.  The displayed identity proves every appended bound.
Thus every genuine counterexample extends to the strengthened CNF.

For efficiency the wrapper omits a bound when the largest possible
sum of that many entries of the selected histogram already lies below
the bound.  The number of positions can be further bounded by
2^(r_max-1), since a linear functional is either zero on the affine
direction space or nonzero on exactly half its vectors.  This omission
only weakens the formula and does not affect one-way soundness.  It
does not assert that every retained row is logically independent of
all previous constraints.

This family addresses the arrangement of near-maximal directions.
For example, the weight-eight, energy-seventy histogram has seven
directions with three pairs and seven with one pair.  Six directions
with three pairs cannot all lie on the nonzero side of one linear
functional: their contribution would be18>16.  A histogram by itself
does not record that obstruction.

No new support exclusion is implied by this lemma or its local replay.
A whole-case exclusion requires a checked refutation of its exact CNF.
