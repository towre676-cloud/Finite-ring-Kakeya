The Sharp Kakeya Law over Four-Element Rings

Exact values through dimension 3, a defect-metric relaxation that is tight where the pair-budget relaxation fails, and the complete equality geometry

Every statement below carries one of 4 epistemic labels. Theorem marks a statement proved in this text by hand. Certified marks a statement established by exhaustive or solver computation in the accompanying package, with every witness re-verified by independent brute-force line enumeration and every infeasibility claim obtained from 2 independent encodings or solvers where so stated. Conjecture marks what remains open. Observed marks solver output that is not a proof.

1Setting

Let $R\in\{\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,\ \mathbb F_2[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\}$, with uniformiser $\pi=2$ or $\pi=\varepsilon$, so $R/\pi R=\mathbb F_2$ and $R^\times=\{1,1+\pi\}$. Write $V=\mathbb F_2^n$ and encode $x\in R^n$ as $x=a+\pi u$ with binary representatives $a,u\in V$; in $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ addition is $(a_1+2u_1)+(a_2+2u_2)=(a_1\oplus a_2)+2(u_1\oplus u_2\oplus a_1\odot a_2)$, in the dual numbers the carry term is absent. A direction is a primitive vector $b=v+\pi w$ with $v\neq 0$, taken modulo $R^\times$; since $(1+\pi)(v+\pi w)=v+\pi(w+v)$, directions are the pairs $(v,[w])$ with $[w]\in V/\langle v\rangle$, numbering $2^{n-1}(2^n-1)$. A Kakeya set is $K\subseteq R^n$ containing a line $\{x+tb:t\in R\}$ for every direction, and $k(R,n)$ is the least size of one. The target is the law $k(R,n)=3\cdot 2^n-2$.

2The construction

Theorem 2.1. $K_*=\{\pi u:u\in V\}\cup\{v,\ v+\pi v: 0\neq v\in V\}$ is a Kakeya set of size $3\cdot2^n-2$ in both rings.

For $b=v+\pi w$ take $x=\pi w$. The points at $t=0,\pi$ lie in the zero fibre; at $t=1$ the point is $v+2\pi w=v$ in both rings (in $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, $2w+2w=4w=0$); at $t=1+\pi$ it is $v+\pi v$. Certified: membership was rechecked by enumeration of all lines for $n\le 5$ in both rings.

3The fibre covering identity

Put $A_a=\{u: a+\pi u\in K\}$, $P_v(A)=\{[u]\in V/\langle v\rangle: u,u+v\in A\}$ and $p_a(v)=|P_v(A_a)|=\tfrac12|A_a\cap(A_a+v)|$. A line of direction $(v,[w])$ through $a+\pi u$ meets exactly the fibres $a$ and $a+v$, in the pairs $\{u,u+v\}$ and $\{u',u'+v\}$ with $u'=u+w+c_R(a,v)$, where $c_R=0$ for the dual numbers and $c_R(a,v)=a\odot v$ for $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$; replacing $a$ by $a+v$ changes $c_R$ by $v\equiv 0$, so the identity is symmetric in the fibre pair.

Theorem 3.1. $K$ is Kakeya if and only if for every $v\neq0$ one has $\bigcup_{\{a,a+v\}}\bigl(P_v(A_a)+P_v(A_{a+v})+[c_R(a,v)]\bigr)=V/\langle v\rangle$. Consequently every Kakeya set satisfies the counting inequality $\sum_{\{a,a+v\}}p_a(v)\,p_{a+v}(v)\ge 2^{n-1}$ for each $v\ne0$.

Certified: the identity and brute-force line enumeration agreed on 12,000 random and perturbed subsets for $n\in\{2,3\}$ in both rings, with 0 disagreements.

4Symmetry reductions

Every element of $\mathrm{AGL}_n(\mathbb F_2)$ lifts to an affine automorphism of $R^n$ (lift the matrix entrywise in $\{0,1\}$; its determinant is a unit), which permutes fibres and preserves the Kakeya property. Because $\mathrm{AGL}_n(\mathbb F_2)$ is 3-transitive and the pointwise stabiliser of $0,e_1,\dots,e_{k-1}$ is transitive off $\mathrm{span}(e_1,\dots,e_{k-1})$, one may assume $s_0\ge s_a$ for all $a$, $s_{e_1}\ge s_a$ for $a\neq0$, and $s_{e_k}\ge s_a$ for $a\notin\mathrm{span}(e_1,\dots,e_{k-1})$, where $s_a=|A_a|$. The kernel of reduction acts by $x\mapsto(I+\pi M)x+\pi c$, shifting $A_a$ by $c+Ma$, so one may further take $0\in A_0$ and $0\in A_{e_i}$ when $A_{e_i}\neq\emptyset$. Alternatively, transitivity on directions and points allows one to fix the line $R e_1$ inside $K$. Both reductions are sound and were used in separate encodings.

6Why the counting relaxation fails

The relaxation (PB) keeps only the sizes $s_a$, the exact pair budget $\sum_{v\neq0}p_a(v)=\binom{s_a}{2}$, the cap $p_a(v)\le\lfloor s_a/2\rfloor$, and the counting inequality of Theorem 3.1. It is ring-independent and bounds $k(R,n)$ from below. Certified: its optimum is 10 at $n=2$ but 21 at $n=3$, attained by the profile $(5,4,4,4,4,0,0,0)$, and at $n=4$ the solver exhibits a feasible point of value 36 with profile six fibres of size 6.

The $n=4$ point exposes the incompatibility precisely. Six fibres of size 6 sitting on a Sidon set of $\mathbb F_2^4$ realise each of the 15 directions by exactly 1 fibre pair, so each pair must cover 8 classes with $p_ap_{a+v}=9$, forcing $p_a(v)=3=s_a/2$ in 5 directions per fibre. But $p_A(v)=|A|/2$ means $A+v=A$, so these 5 directions together with $0$ would lie in the stabiliser subgroup of $A$, whose order divides $|A|=6$ and is therefore at most 2. A counting relaxation cannot see that perfect pairings in different directions compose. The repair must encode composition.

7The defect metric

Theorem 7.1 (fibre defect inequality). For $A\subseteq V$ with $|A|=s$ define $d_A(v)=|A\setminus(A+v)|=s-2p_A(v)$. Then $d_A(v+w)\le d_A(v)+d_A(w)$ for all $v,w$, together with $0\le d_A(v)\le\min(s,2^n-s)$, $d_A(v)\equiv s \pmod 2$ and $\sum_{v\neq0}d_A(v)=s(2^n-s)$.

Proof. $2d_A(v)=|A\,\triangle\,(A+v)|$ and the symmetric difference is a metric on subsets, so $|A\triangle(A+v+w)|\le|A\triangle(A+v)|+|(A+v)\triangle(A+v+w)|$, and the last term equals $|A\triangle(A+w)|$ by translation. The sum identity counts pairs $(u,v)$ with $u\in A$, $u+v\notin A$. $\square$ The stabiliser fact used in §6 is the case $d_A(v)=d_A(w)=0$.

The relaxation (T) adjoins Theorem 7.1 for every fibre to (PB). Every Kakeya set in either ring induces a feasible point of (T), so $\min\mathrm{T}_n\le k(R,n)$ for both rings at once, and (T) forgets the carry entirely.

Certified 7.2. $\min \mathrm T_2=10$ and $\min\mathrm T_3=22$. At $n=3$ the only optimal profile is $(8,2,2,2,2,2,2,2)$, and every point of (T) with all $s_a\le7$ has value at least 24.

So at $n=3$ the single inequality $d(v+w)\le d(v)+d(w)$, applied fibre by fibre, carries the whole sharp law for both rings; positional data within fibres and the carry cocycle are not needed for the lower bound. This is the universal fibre inequality the program asked for, in its first verified form.

8Equality geometry

Theorem 8.1. Suppose that every Kakeya set of size $3\cdot2^n-2$ has fibre profile $(2^n,2,\dots,2)$. Then the extremal sets are exactly $K=\pi V+a_0\ \cup\ \{a+\pi u_a,\ a+\pi(u_a+a-a_0): a\neq a_0\}$, with $a_0\in V$ and $u_a\in V$ arbitrary, and there are exactly $2^n\cdot 2^{(n-1)(2^n-1)}$ of them in each ring.

Proof. Translate so that the full fibre is $A_0$. For $a\neq0$, direction $a$ receives at most $2^{n-1}p_a(a)$ classes from the pair $\{0,a\}$ and at most 1 from each of the $2^{n-1}-1$ remaining pairs of 2-point fibres, so $p_a(a)\ge1$ and $A_a=\{u_a,u_a+a\}$. Conversely $P_a(A_0)=V/\langle a\rangle$, so the sumset in Theorem 3.1 is already everything, whatever the carry. The count is $2^n$ choices of $a_0$ times $2^{n-1}$ classes $[u_a]$ for each of $2^n-1$ fibres, each class giving 2 representatives of the same pair. $\square$

Certified 8.2. The hypothesis of Theorem 8.1 holds for $n\le3$ (Certified 7.2), so there are exactly 32 extremal sets at $n=2$ and $131{,}072$ at $n=3$ in each ring. The $n=2$ count was confirmed by exhaustive census in both rings: 32 and 32, all with profile $(4,2,2,2)$.

This answers the equality questions in the range where they are settled. Optimality forces total concentration into one fibre; there is a single extremal mechanism; the 2 rings share both the minimum and the equality family, because a full fibre absorbs every carry class. The arithmetic difference between $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ and the dual numbers is invisible at the extremum and can only matter strictly above it.

9The reduced conjecture

Conjecture 9.1 (defect form). For all $n$, the minimum of (T) is $3\cdot2^n-2$: if $s_a\in[0,2^n]$ and $d_a:V\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ satisfy the conditions of Theorem 7.1 for every $a\in V$ and $\sum_{\{a,a+v\}}\tfrac14(s_a-d_a(v))(s_{a+v}-d_{a+v}(v))\ge2^{n-1}$ for every $v\neq0$, then $\sum_a s_a\ge 3\cdot2^n-2$.

Conjecture 9.1 implies the sharp law for both rings simultaneously, and it is a statement about subadditive functions on $\mathbb F_2^n$ with no ring left in it. Its proof would need to show that subadditivity forbids medium fibres from serving many directions at once: a fibre with $d_A$ small on a set $D$ has $d_A$ at most twice as large on $D+D$, so many cheap directions in one fibre generate an approximate subgroup and therefore force size, while the pair budget prevents a small fibre from serving more than $\binom{s}{2}$ directions. Every relaxation solution found so far that beats $3\cdot2^n-2$ under (PB) is killed by exactly this mechanism. The decisive computational test is $n=5$, where a point of (T) of value at most 93 would refute Conjecture 9.1 without refuting the ring law, and an actual Kakeya set of at most 93 points would refute both.

10Reproduction

The package kakeya_sharp contains rings.py (exact bit arithmetic, line enumeration, Theorem 3.1 checker), sat_decide.py (CaDiCaL line encoding), exact_sym.py (CP-SAT with the §4 reductions), relaxation.py ((PB) and (T)), profiles.py (equality-profile enumeration) and equality_n2.py (census). Every JSON in results/ records solver, status, wall time and, for feasible outcomes, a witness rechecked by enumeration. Solver UNSAT claims are not accompanied by DRAT proofs in this release; the $n=3$ lower bound is supported by 2 independent encodings and 2 independent solvers.
